The Anarchy at Samarra is a political and military crisis within the Haqq-Caliphate Empire of Abbasiyya, beginning in 128.861 AH following the assassination of Haqq-Caliph Mutawakkil by a group of ghilman.

His death shattered imperial authority as rival ghilman commanders and noble houses began competing to install their preferred candidate as the next Haqq-Caliph.

Background

Abbasiyya is a vast interstellar empire extending approximately 53 light-years across the Lalupa Capitolina Nebula. Its capital, Samarra, is the seat of the Haqq-Caliph and imperial court.

Under Mutawakkil, corruption and political repression spread through the court. His rule alienated the military establishment, particularly the ghilman: an elite class of enslaved soldiers who had accumulated considerable wealth, education, and influence.

Assassination

In 128.861 AH, a group of ghilman publicly assassinated Mutawakkil. The conspirators sought to end his rule but failed to establish a common successor or government.

The imperial court quickly divided due to military commanders and noble families brutal fightings. Each faction began gathering supporters to influence over the vacant throne.

Factions

The ghilman are divided among rival commanders, each commanding their own troops and political alliances. Though united by their opposition to Mutawakkil, they disagree over the empire's future.

The noble houses are equally divided. Some seek to restore the old order, while others support rival claimants in exchange for wealth and political power.

Neither side possesses unified leadership. The conflict is a struggle for the caliphate rather than a single revolution.

Current situation

ARCHIVE STATUS // 128.861 AH
No claimant has secured uncontested control of Samarra or the imperial government.

Samarra remains the centre of the struggle. Alliances shift between military commanders and noble houses as they compete for the throne. Neighbouring powers watch the empire's weakening borders with growing interest.

The future of Abbasiyya remains uncertain.
